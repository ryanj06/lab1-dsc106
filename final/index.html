The Death of the Midrange

How analytics changed the way the NBA scores.

By Tijil Chhabra & team · Data: NBA Games Dataset (Nathan Lauga, Kaggle)

Twenty years ago, an NBA offense lived in the midrange: long two-point jumpers, post-ups, and pull-ups from the elbow. Then the analytics movement asked a simple question — which shots are actually worth the most points? The answer reshaped the league. This explorable explanation lets you move through the seasons and watch the three-point shot take over modern basketball.

Prototype Writeup

What we’ve done so far

We started from the NBA Games Dataset by Nathan Lauga on Kaggle, focusing on games_details.csv, which holds over 600,000 player-game rows of offensive statistics. Because that file is far too large to ship to the browser, we aggregated it down to season-level league averages — three-point attempts per game, three-point share of all field-goal attempts, and points per shot — plus a per-champion summary of three-point volume. We then rebuilt our static matplotlib explorations as interactive D3.js charts: a metric-switching line chart with a season scrubber, and a champions bar chart with hover tooltips. This prototype establishes the data pipeline, the page structure, and the core interaction patterns we will reuse across the final explorable explanation.

The most challenging design aspect

The hardest design decision has been telling a clear story without burying the user in 600,000 rows or a wall of text. We had to choose which aggregations actually carry the “death of the midrange” narrative and which interactions make that change feel tangible rather than decorative. Scrubbing through seasons, for example, only works if the axes stay fixed so the eye tracks the real trend instead of a rescaling artifact. We also wrestled with how much to pre-compute server-side versus in the browser, since responsiveness depends on small, tidy data. Going forward, our biggest challenge will be layering player- and team-level comparisons on top of these league trends without overwhelming the casual basketball fan.
