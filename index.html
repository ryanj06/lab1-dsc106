The Death of
the Midrange

How analytics rewired the NBA's brain and killed the game's most elegant shot.

Tijil Chhabra · Ryan Jose · Leo Wiemelt  ·  Data: NBA Games Dataset (Kaggle)

scroll to explore

Twenty years ago, an NBA offense lived in the midrange. Long two-point jumpers, post-ups, pull-ups from the elbow. That was just how basketball was played. Then the analytics movement asked a simple question: which shots are actually worth the most points?

The answer changed everything. Scroll through the eras below and watch the three-point shot slowly, then all at once, take over the entire sport.

Era 1

The Midrange Reigned

Teams shot ~15 threes per game. Kobe, Duncan, Dirk ruled the elbow.

2003 – 2010

The Midrange Reigned

The NBA ran on post-ups, pull-ups, and long twos. Three-pointers were a bonus, not a strategy. The midrange jumper was art.

14.9threes per game in 2003
2011 – 2014

Analytics Enter the Chat

Moneyball thinking spread to basketball. Front offices started tracking expected points per shot. The midrange was exposed as the worst shot on the floor, lowest return, highest cost.

+43%rise in 3PA from 2003
2015 – 2017

Curry Changes Everything

Steph Curry broke the game. The 2015 Warriors won a championship firing 31 threes a game, a number that would have seemed absurd a decade earlier. Every front office scrambled to copy them.

31.3threes/game — 2015 champions
2018 – 2021

The New Normal

The three-pointer became the default. Teams that resisted fell behind. The midrange was not just unfashionable anymore. It was basically gone.

40.1%of all shots were threes in 2021
