The Death of the Midrange

How analytics changed the way the NBA scores.

By Tijil Chhabra (tchhabra@ucsd.edu), Ryan Jose (rjose@ucsd.edu) & Leo Wiemelt (lwiemelt@ucsd.edu)

Data: NBA Games Dataset (Nathan Lauga, Kaggle)

Twenty years ago, an NBA offense lived in the midrange: long two-point jumpers, post-ups, and pull-ups from the elbow. Then the analytics movement asked a simple question — which shots are actually worth the most points? The answer reshaped the league. This explorable explanation lets you move through the seasons and watch the three-point shot take over modern basketball.

Prototype Writeup

What we’ve done so far

The prototype originated with the NBA Games Dataset provided by Nathan Lauga on Kaggle; specifically, the games_details.csv dataset, containing over 600,000 rows of offensive statistics for players per game. Given that this CSV is too large to transmit to the browser, we reduced the data set to league-level metrics per season, namely, average three-point attempts per game, average percentage of shots attempted from beyond the arc among all field goal attempts, and average points scored per shot. In addition, we added a summary metric for each champion, regarding the amount of three-point shooting. Finally, we recreated the static matplotlib graphs as dynamic D3.js graphs, with an option to switch between metrics on the first and a champions bar chart on the second graph, including tooltip functionality.

The most challenging design aspect

The most difficult design choice, however, was communicating a coherent narrative without drowning the user in 600,000 data points or an endless amount of text. Decisions had to be made regarding what kind of aggregations were truly illustrative of the “death of the midrange” story, and what interactions conveyed its significance rather than merely being superficial. Browsing by seasons, for instance, only really makes sense if the axes are locked down and allow one to perceive the real trend, not an illusion due to axis rescaling. We also grappled with how far pre-computation should be taken server-side versus client-side, as responsiveness required manageable and clean data. The next major challenge facing us is combining individual and team-level analysis with this league-wide trend.
